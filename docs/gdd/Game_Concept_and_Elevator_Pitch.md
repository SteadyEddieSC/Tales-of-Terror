# Game Concept and Elevator Pitch

**Working title:** Terror Turn
**Host:** The Underteller
**Status:** Both names are provisional pending issue #7
**Current runtime:** Internal vertical slice with Lantern House as the sole production Tale

## Core concept

Terror Turn is a controller-first digital horror board game for 1–8 local players sharing one television. Players enter authored storybook-horror scenarios called **Tales**, explore a changing board, resolve deterministic events and checks, manage items and clues, and make public or private decisions that can change objectives, alliances, factions, or player forms.

The game is built around social horror rather than simple elimination. A player may begin as an ally, reveal a hidden agenda, become the Horror, join a Changed faction, or continue after defeat as a Restless witness, wraith, guardian, minion, or replacement investigator. Optional companion devices may provide private information, but the complete local game must remain playable without phones.

The fictional host, **The Underteller**, presents the story and reacts to public consequences. A separate deterministic Director adjusts authored pacing within strict privacy and fairness boundaries. The Director cannot inspect unrevealed roles, private objectives, hidden targets, private messages, or pending transformations.

## Fifteen-second elevator pitch

Terror Turn is a 1–8 player couch horror board game where friends explore a living storybook world, alliances can change mid-story, and defeat gives you a new role instead of removing you from the game.

## Thirty-second elevator pitch

Terror Turn turns the living room into a haunted digital board game for up to eight players. Each authored Tale has its own mystery, board, monsters, roles, and endings. Players cooperate, bargain, conceal information, and survive a signature Terror Turn that may transform the board or the group itself. The Underteller hosts the story, an adaptive rule-based Director manages the pressure, and defeated players return through meaningful Restless or transformed roles rather than sitting out.

## One-hundred-word pitch

Gather 1–8 players around one television and open a dangerous illustrated board game. In Terror Turn, each Tale is a self-contained horror story with its own shifting board, mysteries, monsters, social roles, transformations, and endings. Explore together, collect clues and items, resolve events, and decide when to trust, bargain, reveal, or betray. The Underteller frames the story while a deterministic Director adjusts authored pressure without reading hidden information. A signature Terror Turn can flood the board, change the objective, expose a faction, or transform a player. Defeat rarely ends participation; the dead and Changed keep shaping the finale.

## Story mode pitch: Tales

A **Tale** is the game’s story mode: a complete authored horror adventure designed like a board-game scenario and a short interactive campaign combined.

Every Tale can define:

- a distinct location and visual identity;
- a staged story arc;
- an explorable Living Board;
- clues, items, cards, hazards, and encounters;
- public and private roles;
- cooperative, betrayal, faction, transformation, or survival structures;
- one or more Terror Turns;
- Restless or transformed continuation after defeat;
- several possible endings and mixed outcomes;
- Tale-specific dialogue, music, sound, captions, and accessible presentation.

Tales may be played as standalone sessions or linked into a Chronicle Campaign when authored persistence is supported. A Tale does not need to support every mode or seat structure. Unsupported configurations must use a deliberate cooperative or no-secret fallback rather than producing an invalid social layout.

## What makes the concept distinctive

### A living board, not a static map

The board is an authoritative game system. It can reveal, flood, burn, collapse, rotate, split, block routes, expose hazards, or change objectives while remaining deterministic and replayable.

### Social horror with bounded privacy

Fear comes from uncertainty about the board and the people at the table. Public, seat-private, faction-private, and controlled-private information are separated intentionally. The shared television remains neutral during private handoffs.

### The Terror Turn

A Terror Turn is a major authored reversal, not every ordinary turn. It may change the board, objective, allegiance, faction, or player form and should create a memorable before-and-after moment.

### Meaningful afterlife

Defeat normally opens a new participation path. Restless players may warn, haunt, testify, protect, manipulate the board, serve the Horror, or return through an authored replacement role.

### A host who tells the truth about the rules

The Underteller may be theatrical, ominous, or sarcastic, but presentation cannot misstate legal actions, outcomes, privacy, or authority. Mechanical truth outranks atmosphere.

### Adaptive pacing without hidden-state cheating

The Director can respond to allowlisted public and aggregate signals, but it cannot inspect private plans. Its choices remain authored, deterministic, bounded, and explainable.

## Current-state pitch

The repository currently contains a functional internal vertical slice rather than a finished commercial game. Lantern House is the only production Tale and proves the full route from lobby and private reveals through explicit interactions, ending, and rematch. Drowned Harbor is design-only and has only an export-excluded development-isolation manifest.

## Future-state pitch

The intended finished game is a multi-Tale storybook-horror platform with standalone sessions, linked chronicles, multiple social structures, shifting boards, optional private companion devices, presentation profiles, original visual and audio identity, and enough authored variation that each Tale feels like opening a different haunted board game.

## Drowned Harbor example pitch

At an impossible low tide, a drowned town rises from black tidal mud. Travelers cross a broken causeway toward a Bellhouse, Salt Market, lifeboat shed, flooded archive, and distant lighthouse. The town’s leaders once wrecked ships and erased the harbor from memory through a ledger-and-bell ritual, but one missing name left the ritual incomplete. As the sea returns, the group must recover the truth, survive the High Water Terror Turn, and decide whether the harbor is sealed, released, raised, abandoned, or remembered.

Drowned Harbor is currently a future Tale in design. It is not selectable, production-registered, exported, balanced, or human-validated.
